# Explanation: build understanding

Answer a bounded conceptual question. Connect ideas, explain constraints, and
show their implications for the reader's mental model.

## Writing shape

- Name the subject or question and establish why it matters to this audience.
- Introduce the central model and the relationships between its parts.
- Use concrete scenarios or useful comparisons to make the model intelligible.
- Discuss relevant trade-offs, alternatives, and limitations.
- Link to task guides for action and reference for exact behavior.

Rationale and history belong here when supported by evidence and appropriate for
customers. Separate established behavior from interpretation; do not invent why a
team made a choice. Translate approved internal rationale into customer-relevant
language rather than exposing internal debates or copying a decision record.

Keep the page about its subject. A numbered walkthrough or exhaustive field table
usually belongs in another mode. A small illustrative example can stay when it
helps explain a relationship.

## Review test

Can the reader explain the concept, its boundaries, and a meaningful consequence
without following a procedure? Keep explanation a current conceptual guide, not a
replacement for an internal ADR or a proposal for future behavior.

Illustrative title: **How imports handle duplicates**.

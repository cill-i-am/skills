# Research

Frame the decision using the user's context, constraints, dates, and relevant versions. If asked only for a research brief, provide that brief without running the investigation; include the question, known context, source expectations, and desired result.

For research, use primary sources for material claims and inspect the actual page, paper, code, or data. Use secondary sources to find leads or understand perspectives. Verify volatile facts and implementation-sensitive claims against current sources and installed versions.

Follow the evidence until the decision is supported or the important uncertainty is clear. Resolve contradictions and corroborate consequential claims where possible. Do not prescribe a fixed number of searches or pad the answer with low-quality sources.

Lead with the answer and cite the supporting sources near the claims. Distinguish fact, inference, and unresolved uncertainty. Save a report when requested or useful for substantial work; choose its structure for the reader rather than a mandatory template.
